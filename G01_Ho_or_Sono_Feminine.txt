Grammar lesson one.

Ho or sono?

(pause: 2)

(voice: Jessica)

(narration-language: en-US)

Practise choosing the helping verb in the Italian past tense called passato prossimo.

We will use sixteen common verbs, always speaking about I.

The model answers are spoken as a woman, with endings such as arrivata.

Changes of person and gender belong to a separate lesson.

Here, concentrate on choosing the right helping verb.

(pause: 1)

(voice: Jessica)

(narration-language: en-US)

Italian builds this past tense from two parts.

First comes a form of avere or essere, the helping verb.

Then comes the past participle.

Listen to two examples.

(pause: 1)

(voice: Alessandra)

(narration-language: it-IT)

Ho mangiato.

(pause: 2)

(voice: Alessandra)

(narration-language: it-IT)

Sono arrivata.

(pause: 2)

(voice: Jessica)

(narration-language: en-US)

Both describe past events.

Ho mangiato means I ate, or I have eaten.

Sono arrivata means I arrived, or I have arrived.

Do not translate sono literally as am in this construction.

Learn the whole past-tense phrase.
